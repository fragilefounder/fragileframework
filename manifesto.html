The ritual and the work.

Agile gave teams useful ways to inspect work, learn, and adapt. Those ideas need more than a board and a calendar. People need clear decisions, evidence they can question, and room to act on what they learn.

frAGILE looks at the distance between the ritual and the work. Below are four familiar Agile values after a trip through the org chart. The distortions are ours, not quotations from the original Manifesto for Agile Software Development.

Section I / Values

Four values, distorted.

Each familiar failure has a practical answer beneath it.

I

Individuals and interactions

The distortion

Everyone leaves the meeting feeling heard. The decision leaves with nobody.

What holds up

Name the decision, who makes it, who takes the next step, and what support is needed. Kindness includes clarity.

II

Working software

The distortion

The document is approved. The product is still a promise.

What holds up

Record the decision, its rationale, and the constraints the next person must respect. Documentation earns its place when it helps someone act or protects quality. Show the working thing, too.

III

Customer collaboration

The distortion

The loudest request wins, then gets described as customer-led.

What holds up

Bring current customer evidence into the decision. If safety, policy, or other constraints point elsewhere, record the trade-off and who made it.

IV

Responding to change

The distortion

Every change is called a pivot. Nobody can name the evidence that moved the plan.

What holds up

Name the new evidence, the work that stops, who decides, and when the new direction will be reviewed.

Section II / Principles

Six principles worth keeping.

Short enough to remember. Specific enough to test.

  1. 01

    A polished status update cannot make unfinished work usable.Show the work.

  2. 02

    Before making feedback actionable, make it safe to give.

  3. 03

    When the burndown is green and the team is worried, investigate the gap.

  4. 04

    A pivot should retire some work.Otherwise it is a new label on the same backlog.

  5. 05

    Culture appears when someone brings bad news.Watch what happens next.

  6. 06

    Every blocker has a story.Listen before fixing.

Next step

Name the pattern.
Change the work.

No certificates. No maturity model. No transformational lanyard. Pick one recurring practice this week and ask what decision, learning, or protection it is meant to provide. If the answer is unclear, make its purpose explicit or change the practice. Name who will take the change forward and when the team will check whether it helped.
