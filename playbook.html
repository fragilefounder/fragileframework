Chapter I

Ceremonies

Four rituals investigated. Attendance was excellent.

Case 001Sprint planning

The Sprint That Planned Itself

Signal
“We’ll figure out scope during the sprint.” The board has work, but the team cannot name a shared outcome.
What keeps it going
The team is asked to commit to tickets before an open decision is made.
Try
Agree on the sprint goal and what can wait. Put the unresolved choice with the person who can make it before treating the list as a commitment.
Look for
Can three teammates describe the same goal and what would make it change?
Case 002Daily standup

The Standup That Would Not End

Signal
“Yesterday I... Today I... No blockers.” The room gets an update; the plan stays untouched.
What keeps it going
The meeting rewards a complete recital instead of useful coordination.
Try
Ask what changed since yesterday, what is at risk, and who needs to coordinate. Move detailed problem-solving to the people involved.
Look for
Did anyone get unblocked or adjust the plan?
Case 003Sprint retrospective

The Retro That Solved Nothing

Signal
The same concern appears on new sticky notes. Nothing changes by the next sprint.
What keeps it going
Concerns are collected, but no change is chosen. Some may be difficult to raise safely in the room.
Try
Choose one change within the team’s reach, name who will take it forward, and check it at the next retro. Route concerns that need outside authority without asking someone to defend a sensitive issue in public.
Look for
Was the change tried? Did it help?
Case 004Sprint review

The Demo That Demoed a Prototype of a Demo

Signal
“Please imagine this is clickable.” A deck presents a prototype as delivered work.
What keeps it going
The review is expected to look finished, so the difference between completed work and learning gets blurred.
Try
Show completed work as completed, prototypes as experiments, and unresolved work as unresolved. Use the review to decide what to adapt next.
Look for
Did the review reveal an assumption or change a decision?
Chapter II

Roles and incentives

The role gets a title. The system decides what it can do.

Case 005Product Owner and customer evidence

The Customer Voice on Mute

Signal
A decision is made on behalf of customers, but no current customer evidence reaches the team.
What keeps it going
Research, support, frontline, and direct customer signals have no reliable path into planning. One role is left to improvise “the voice.”
Try
Bring one relevant, recent customer signal to the decision. Mark what remains an assumption and how the consequential unknowns could be tested.
Look for
Did the evidence change the choice or clarify what is still unknown?
Case 006Scrum Master and team ceremonies

The Ceremony That Outlived Its Reason

Signal
Everyone attends. Nobody can say what the meeting should decide, learn, or protect.
What keeps it going
The calendar invite has an owner; its purpose does not.
Try
State the expected output and any quality or governance need it serves. Shorten or redesign the meeting. Retire it if it is optional and serves no distinct purpose.
Look for
Did the team preserve the needed decision, record, or safeguard with less time?
Case 007Delivery Lead and outcome measures

The Release That Became the Result

Signal
The release is counted as success. Nobody checks whether people can use what shipped, or whether it helped.
What keeps it going
Delivery is easier to report than the outcome the work was meant to improve.
Try
Pair release status with one meaningful use or outcome signal. Set a date to inspect it, even if the first measure is an imperfect proxy.
Look for
Did the signal change the next investment or reveal a question worth investigating?
Case 008Stakeholder access and scope decisions

The Override That Arrived in Sprint Six

Signal
A late request changes the plan. Nobody names what now waits.
What keeps it going
Stakeholders and risk partners entered after the trade-offs became expensive, and decision rights remained vague.
Try
Record who can change priority, what new evidence or obligation warrants it, and which work moves out. Welcome late customer, safety, or regulatory facts. Make their cost and importance visible.
Look for
Can the team explain why the change was made and what it displaced?
Chapter III

Tools and templates

Four artifacts recovered. None can speak for itself.

Case 009Definition of Done

The Poster That Time Forgot

Signal
The criteria are on the wall. The team does not use them to decide whether work is complete.
What keeps it going
The criteria were not revisited as the team, product, and requirements changed.
Try
Review them with the team and anyone accountable for quality or required controls. Retain required safeguards, revise obsolete steps, and use the result on the next item.
Look for
Does completed work meet shared criteria without a last-minute surprise?
Case 010Burndown chart

The Chart Nobody Read

Signal
The chart is produced every sprint and opened only after the deadline slips.
What keeps it going
It appears in reporting, but no decision uses it.
Try
For the next two sprints, ask what decision the chart changes. If it changes none, retire it or replace it with a signal the team can use.
Look for
Did it help the team see a risk early enough to respond?
Case 011Velocity

The Score That Was Not a Score

Signal
Story points are used to rank teams or assess a person's performance.
What keeps it going
A planning estimate became a target, changing what the number rewards.
Try
Use velocity, if useful, for planning within the team. Keep story points out of individual appraisal. Consider delivery alongside evidence of quality and value.
Look for
Do estimates help the team plan without rewarding point inflation?
Case 012Shared work board

The Board That Never Moved

Signal
Most work sits in “In Progress” or “Blocked.” The standup proceeds from the board as if it were current.
What keeps it going
The board serves upward reporting more reliably than team coordination.
Try
Ask the people doing the work what is moving and what is stuck. Update the shared board together. Name the decision or support each blocked item needs.
Look for
Can someone use the board to help sooner?
Chapter IV / Findings

Four truths recovered.

Twelve cases. The patterns beneath them.

  1. 01

    Rituals need a job.

    A ceremony should make a decision clearer, expose a risk, or help the team learn. Attendance is not an outcome.

  2. 02

    Roles need the conditions to succeed.

    Customer access, decision rights, and permission to raise a concern cannot be supplied by a job title. Fix the condition before blaming the person.

  3. 03

    Artifacts need to match reality.

    A board, chart, or Definition of Done earns its place when people can use it to see progress, risk, or quality while there is time to act.

  4. 04

    A familiar joke is a clue.

    When everyone recognizes the pattern, ask what rewards it, what makes it hard to challenge, and who can change one condition. Then check what happened.
