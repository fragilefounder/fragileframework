01 / Origin

Why frAGILE exists

If a video brought you here, hello. I make the satire and build the tools you’ll find on this site.

frAGILE looks at the rituals, language, and incentives that can make a busy organization look effective while the actual work gets harder. The humor is aimed at those systems and patterns. People trying to do good work inside them deserve some generosity.

The project includes videos, a dictionary, a manifesto, a playbook, and a Readiness Check. Some give a familiar problem a name. Others help you examine a piece of work or start a more useful conversation with your team.

02 / Experience

The work behind it

Business analysis, product management, and product leadership.

My background spans startup and enterprise environments, working on software for complicated, regulated transactions. That has included digital title, mortgage technology, electronic closing, and digital notarization.

First American Digital Title Group

I led product managers and business analysts and helped build a SaaS transaction platform and an automated title-search MVP. I also entered test orders myself and evaluated the results.

Stewart

I led a cross-functional product organization on a title and escrow production system. The work connected product, design and research, training, and operations across workflows, integrations, and release readiness.

Those are the kinds of practical problems behind this project: understanding the workflow, making decisions clear, and checking whether something actually works for the people who use it.

03 / Working approach

Clarity, with room for people

I take the work seriously. Under pressure, I aim to stay calm, be honest about what we know, and help people work through the next decision. Clear expectations and explicit ownership matter. People need to know what decision they can make and when to ask for help. I value hearing bad news early enough to do something about it.

Developing product managers has been part of my work too: one-on-ones, coaching, and helping people build their own judgment. I’ve worked with a leadership coach myself. There’s plenty to keep learning.

That perspective carries into frAGILE. A useful process should help people understand the problem, make a decision, or test what they’re assuming.
